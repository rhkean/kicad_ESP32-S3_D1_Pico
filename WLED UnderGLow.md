# WLED UnderGlow: Project Master Record

##  Project Summary
The WLED UnderGlow is an automotive-grade lighting controller designed on 2 dense 30x30mm 6-layer PCB's that sit on top of a main 3rd party power/fuse board, the QuinLED DigUno. The 2 PCB's are independently referred to as a MCU hat and I/O hat. The design features an ESP32-S3-Pico-1 brain with integrated CAN FD connectivity via the TCAN4550-Q1. The system is built for extreme efficiency and reliability, featuring sophisticated power muxing, high-side current sensing, and a multi-stage sleep cycle to protect the vehicle's battery.

### MCU Hat
- 6 layer 30x30mm PCB from JLCPCB based on the QuinLED-ESP32 (D1 Mini32) footprint.
- The right and left sides of the board are populated with 2x10 header pins with 2.54 pitch to facilitate board stacking and the reduce space needed for the system.
- L1 consists of the EPS32-S3-PICO-1, 0402 chip antenna, 2 LEDs to indicate power source, 1 LED to show activity (on GPIO2), and 2 small SPST-NO buttons.  One is for Reset and the other to allow manual entry into Boot-mode.
- L6 consists of an advanced power mux (TPS2121) to safely support 5V external power from the main board or $V_{BUS}$ from the USB-C connector.  The power mux is configured to provide continuous 5V power while limiting the electronic system to a safe level 1.2A.  It is also designed with a Schottky diode to protect against reverse battery polarity.  It then feeds the 5V supply to a TPSM82903 integrated buck converter to generate a 3V3 power rail for the system.  There is also a LED to indicate reverse polarity.

### I/O Hat
- contains CAN-FD bus integrated transceiver/controller (TPS4550). The controller is configured with a watchdog timer that can reboot the system, including itself (a suicide switch), if needed.
- 5 $V_{BAT}$-level signal inputs for L/R turn signals, Reverse, Brakes, and interior Dome light.  The inputs are active high and lowered to safe value for the 74LVC1G14 inverters with a 100k/26k1 voltage divider.  The dome signal, however, is active-low. This signal uses a diode steering circuit combined with another inverter to buffer the signal to the mcu and prevent current leakage when the signal is high.
- Another inverter is used to toggle the TPS4550's reset signal since its RST pin is active high.
- A dual load switch (TPS2HC08) is implemented for controlling power to the interior lights and the engine bay bosch relay for the exterior lights.  The ground signal from the relay is tied to a dedicated "dirty-ground" trace to 1 of the 4 GND pins on the board.
- 6 data output lines are level shifted to 5V (4.5V actually) with 75LV1T126 buffers. The data signals for the SK6812, WS2811, and WS2814 LED signals are generated by RMT signals from the ESP32-S3 mcu. Each signal is sent to 1 of each of the LED strips and paired with a clean data ground in a twisted pair implementation.

##  Major Design Decisions
- MCU hat - Layer 5 (L5) Zoning: To handle high density without a full power plane, L5 is split into three zones: Antenna Ground (Top 1/5), 3.3V Power Pour (Middle 2/5), and Internal Power Bus (Bottom 2/5).
- Power Muxing (TPS2121): Implements Fast Switchover (~5µs) between USB and Battery. It uses a 0.65V ground lift via a Schottky diode for reverse polarity protection, with all logic ladders adjusted for this offset. This only impacts the TPS2121 and its related components... NOT the rest of the board
- Hybrid Input Protection: 
    - L/R Turn, Brake, Reverse , & Dome: Use 74LVC1G14 gates as sacrificial barriers.
- Dome Light Logic: A custom Diode-Isolated Pull-Up circuit was engineered to eliminate continuous current bleed when doors are closed.
- Diagnostics: A Global Health Bus (Wired-OR) will tie the Buck PG, Load Switch $\overline{\text{FLT}}$, and CAN $\overline{\text{INT}}$ pins to a single interrupt pin.

- CAN FD: TCAN4550-Q1
- Power Mux: TPS2121-Q1
- High-Side Switch: TPS2HC08-Q1

##  Current Sense (SNS) Specs
- Configuration: $R_{SNS}$ (1.62kΩ) tied to System Ground to utilize full 0–3.3V range.
- Range: 5A Load ≈ 2.68V typical.
- Fault Logic: Anything reading 3.3V (Clipped) on the ADC is treated as a hardware fault (short circuit or thermal event).

# Project Master Techinical Specification: Automotive LED/CAN IO-Hat

## 1. System Architecture & Grounding
* **MCU Hat:** ESP32-S3-Pico-1 based on the QuinLED-ESP32 (D1 Mini32) footprint.
* **Grounding Strategy:**
    * **Clean System GND (0V):** Referenced by the ESP32-S3, Level Shifters, and CAN Transceiver.
    * **Dirty GND:** The **TPS2HC08** (High-Side Switch) and **TPS2121** (Power Mux) are "lifted" via a Schottky diode ($+0.65\text{V}$) for reverse polarity protection. These 2 INDEPENDENT ground lifts are exclusive to the 2 chips and their passive components.
    * **Header Isolation:** One of the four GND pins on the 2x10 header is dedicated to "Dirty GND" (Relay flyback/TPS return). It remains isolated from the "Clean" logic ground on the IO-Hat and is tied only at the main power board star-point.
* **LED Data Returns:** Each of the 6 LED data lines is paired with a GND wire in a twisted-pair configuration, returned specifically to the **Clean System GND** to maintain logic reference.

## 2. LED Output Subsystem
* **Controller:** ESP32-S3 **RMT (Remote Control)** peripheral handles all 6 channels via DMA for precise WS2812B/SK6812 timing.
* **Level Shifting:** 6x **SN74LV1T126** single-gate buffers ($3.3\text{V} \rightarrow 5\text{V}$).
    * **Pull-Downs:** $5.1\text{k}\Omega$ (BOM consolidated) on shifter's OE pins to prevent flickering during MCU High-Z states (boot/reset).
    * **Termination:** **$33\Omega$** series resistors placed at the buffer outputs to dampen reflections on automotive wiring runs.
* **Power:** Level shifters are powered by the internal $5\text{V}$ bus.

## 3. Vehicle Signal Inputs (5 Channels)
* **Signals:** Left Turn, Right Turn, Brakes, Reverse (Active-High) and Dome Light (Active-Low).
* **Conditioning Circuit (per channel):**
    1.  **Voltage Divider:** $100\text{k}\Omega$ (High) and $26.1\text{k}\Omega$ (Low) scaling $12\text{V}$ signals to $\approx 2.4\text{V}$.
    2.  **Filtering:** $0.1\mu\text{F}$ capacitor creates a $\approx 13\text{Hz}$ low-pass filter to reject engine/alternator noise.
    3.  **Dome Light Specifics:** Includes a steering diode before the divider to prevent $12\text{V}$ back-feeding and battery drain.
* **Logic Buffering:**
    * **Component:** **74LVC1G14** (Dual Schmitt-Trigger Inverting Buffers).
    * **Benefit:** The Schmitt trigger provides hysteresis and boosts the $2.4\text{V}$ divided signal safely above the ESP32's $2.4\text{V}$ $V_{IH}$ requirement.
    * **Output:** Push-Pull configuration (eliminates 6 pull-up resistors initially required with original open-drain design).
* **Firmware Logic:** Note that, unless future design decisions change, this configuration means that all signals become **Active-Low** at the MCU except the Dome Light, which becomes **Active-High**.  input gate logic can be set at runtime and does not affect hardware design decisions.

## 4. CAN Bus & Safety
* **Transceiver:** **TCAN4550** (CAN-FD) communicating via SPI.
* **Suicide Switch:** Logic is implemented to allow the TCAN4550 to hard-reset the system via its GPO2 pin and itself by inverting that signal if the MCU fails the watchdog check.
* **Global Health Bus:** GPIO_03 on the mcu will be designated as an open-drain wired-OR fault interrupt solution.  This will be tied to each subsystem that provides an open-drain fault indicator, allowing for system-wide fault monitoring.

## 5. PCB Layout Guidelines (Dual 30x30mm, 6-Layer boards) + QuinLED DigUno
* **Stackup:** from bottom to top
  - **DigUno:** serves as the base/main board of the solution.  Power is provided from the GR86's hidden DCM connector behind the glovebox. Contains 5A fuse to protect system and vehicle.  Converts vehicle $V_{BAT}$ to 5.12V for the PCB "hats".
  - **MCU Hat:** ESP32-S3 with integrated 5V-to-3V3 buck converter, USB-C/JTAG interface, and USB-C prioritzed power mux.
  - **I/O Hat:** 
    - **CAN-FD bus:** CAN signal and vehicle power provided through the glovebox DCM connector. CAN controller monitors bus to wake mcu when needed and provides watchdog to reset system if it hangs
    - **Vehicle Input Signals:** 5 12V signals for L/R Turn Signals, Reverse, Brakes, and Dome light
    - **WLED Output:** 6 LED strip data outputs that are level-shifted to 5V
    - **Power Management:** Load switches to control power to interior LED strips and exterior Bosch-style relay.
    - **Component Placement:** L1 will contain the high density wire-to-board headers needed to interface with the vehicle.  This should allow most electronic components, such as the '14 inverters and '126 buffers to be clustered on L6 below the associated header pins to minimize high-speed trace lengths and improve data signals where needed. There is sufficient space to allow the 20-pin headers to be placed back-to-back at the top of the board in either an East/West orientation or North/South, depending on how the circuitry on L6 is routed. The 10-pin header will be aligned with the bottom edge of the board oriented toward the DCM vehicle connector.
* **BOM Consolidation:** BOM consolidation is a major contributing factor in decision making, because each additional line item adds another $2 to the assembly costs.  Part quantity is not a concern, bc additional parts impact the costs by pennies, where as additional line items impact by dollars.
    * Standardized on $5.1\text{k}\Omega$ pull-down and pull-up dresistors across both boards
    * $100\text{k}\Omega$/$26.1\text{k}\Omega$ resistors for all 12V-to-3V3 down-scaling.

- **$I_{SNS}$ from TCAN:**
```
float getInteriorLightAmps() {
  uint32_t raw = analogRead(SNS_PIN);
  
  // 1. Check for Hardware Fault Signal (Internal 4.5mA Source)
  if (raw > 3800) return -1.0; // Signal for "Chip is in Thermal/Short Protection"

  // 2. Convert ADC to Voltage (assuming 11dB attenuation / 3.1V FSR)
  float voltage = (raw / 4095.0) * 3.1;
  
  // 3. Convert Voltage to SNS Current (I = V/R)
  float i_sns = voltage / 1600.0;
  
  // 4. Convert SNS Current to Load Current (I_out = I_sns * K_sns)
  float i_load = i_sns * 3008.0;
  
  return i_load;
}
```

# Design Constraints
- **MCU Hat:** This board design is complete and alterations involving the GPIO lines are not a viable option. This board is intended to be used as a generic brain board for this and future projects, so any alterations to the GPIO signal lines must be handled on the I/O hat.
- **BOM Consolidation:** BOM consolidation is a major contributing factor in decision making, because each additional line item adds another $2 to the assembly costs.  Part quantity is not a concern, bc additional parts impact the costs by pennies, where as additional line items impact by dollars.
